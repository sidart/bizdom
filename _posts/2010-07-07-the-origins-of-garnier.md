In 1904, when washing one's hair with ordinary soap was still a common practice, Alfred Amour Garnier developed a hair lotion using plant extracts. Marketed in France under the name *Lotion Garnier*, the product laid the foundation of Laboratoires Garnier.

The company gradually expanded its range to include shampoos and other haircare preparations. Advertisements in French women's magazines promoted the benefits of ingredients derived from plants, an association that would remain central to Garnier's product identity.

In 1936, Garnier ventured into sun-care products, at a time when paid holidays and the growing popularity of seaside vacations were creating a market for sunscreen lotions in Europe.

Another commercial opportunity emerged in the 1960s when Garnier introduced permanent hair colouring products that could be applied at home, reducing the dependence on professional hairdressers. In 1965, Laboratoires Garnier was acquired by another French cosmetics manufacturer, L'Oréal, whose own origins lay in a hair dye formulation developed by Eugène Schueller in 1907.

Under L'Oréal, Garnier gradually developed a portfolio of sub-brands. Fructis, introduced in 1996, used fruit extracts as part of its haircare formulations. Nutrisse, its hair colouring range, was originally introduced under the name Natea in 1998 before being marketed under its subsequent name.

In 2005, Garnier introduced Nutritioniste, a skincare range marketed around the nutritional requirements of the skin.

By the time Garnier completed a century of its existence, the plant-based hair lotion introduced by Alfred Garnier had grown into an international cosmetics business covering hair colouring, styling, skincare and sun protection. Its original association with natural ingredients continued to feature prominently in its packaging and advertising.
