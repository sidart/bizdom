In 1922, Lala Arjan Das Kohli established a sports goods manufacturing business called International Sports in Sialkot, a town already known for its sporting goods industry. The firm initially manufactured footballs, rubber bladders and gut strings used in badminton racquets. Following Partition, the family migrated to India and established a manufacturing facility in Meerut in 1950.

The family's association with table tennis began in earnest in 1975, when the factory manufactured a table for Rakesh Kohli, the founder's grandson. In 1981, the family adopted the brand name Stag, taking inspiration from the animal's agility, much like the movements of a table tennis player.

In 1982, Stag introduced rollaway tables in India. The following year, its tables received approval from the Table Tennis Federation of India, opening up a market among state-level sporting associations.

At the 1991 World Table Tennis Championships in Japan, Vivek Kohli offered to supply ten tables free of cost to Uganda under a development programme. The Ugandan federation subsequently placed a commercial order for another 40 tables.

The Kohli brothers also discovered that while their tables were relatively expensive internationally, their net sets, priced at $2 against the prevailing $5, were highly competitive. They began selling the two products together.

By 2011, Stag was exporting sporting equipment to 152 countries. The business founded by Arjan Das and carried forward by his son Tilak Raj had by then entered its fourth generation, with Pranav Kohli joining the family enterprise.
