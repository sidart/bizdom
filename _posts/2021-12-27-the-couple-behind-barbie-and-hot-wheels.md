In 1968, Mattel introduced its first range of Hot Wheels cars, popularly known among collectors as the ‘Sweet 16’. The idea came from Elliot Handler, co-founder of Mattel, who had reportedly noticed his son playing with Matchbox cars, then a popular British toy brand. Elliot wanted to produce miniature cars that looked like customised American automobiles and could run faster on specially designed plastic tracks.

His wife, Ruth Handler, had already given Mattel its most famous product. In 1959, she introduced Barbie, named after their daughter Barbara. Two years later came Ken, named after their son Kenneth.

However, the couple's association with the company they had helped establish in 1945 came to an end in the mid-1970s, following a controversy over Mattel's financial reporting. In 1978, Ruth was indicted on charges related to false financial statements, to which she subsequently pleaded no contest.

The two toy brands created by the Handlers continue to be among Mattel's biggest commercial properties.
