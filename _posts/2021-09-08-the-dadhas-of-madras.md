In Phalodi, a trading town in the Marwar region of Rajasthan, stands the ancestral haveli of the Dadha family, built in 1750. The Dadhas belonged to the Oswal community and had long-standing associations with the royal house of Jodhpur. Like several other mercantile families of Rajasthan, they eventually established business interests in southern India.

S. Mohanchand Dadha was born in Phalodi on 21 October 1936, to S. Lalchand Dadha and Balubai Dadha. He was barely six months old when he was brought to Madras, where he grew up speaking Tamil as naturally as Hindi.

The family's association with the pharmaceutical trade went back to the early twentieth century. The Dadhas initially dealt in medicines, gradually acquiring experience in pharmaceutical distribution and subsequently entering manufacturing. In those days, much of India's requirement for modern medicines was met by foreign pharmaceutical companies, whose products reached Indian consumers through local agents, wholesalers and chemists.

### The Sun Pharma connection

In 1997, Tamil Nadu Dadha Pharmaceuticals became part of Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, a company established by Dilip Shanghvi in 1983 with a portfolio of just five products.

The acquisition gave Sun access to manufacturing and research capabilities in specialised pharmaceutical products, including oncology drugs.

One of the products subsequently developed at the acquired facility was Lipodox, a formulation of doxorubicin encapsulated in microscopic lipid-based particles called liposomes. Johnson & Johnson marketed a similar formulation under the brand name Doxil.

When the United States faced an acute shortage of Doxil in 2011–12, Sun received permission from the US Food and Drug Administration to supply Lipodox. The shortage created a particularly lucrative commercial opportunity for the Indian drugmaker.

Sun's acquisition-led expansion continued, culminating in its purchase of Ranbaxy Laboratories in 2015, which made it India's largest pharmaceutical company.

### The next generation

The Dadhas, meanwhile, continued their association with the pharmaceutical distribution business. In 2015, Pradeep Dadha launched Netmeds, an online pharmacy, drawing upon the family's experience in procuring and distributing medicines.

The emergence of online pharmacies introduced a different set of requirements to the traditional pharmaceutical trade. Medicines had to be catalogued digitally, prescriptions verified remotely and individual orders delivered to customers across the country.

In August 2020, Reliance Retail Ventures announced an investment of approximately ₹620 crore in the Netmeds business, acquiring a majority stake in its parent company, Vitalic Health, along with full ownership of certain subsidiaries.

There is an unusual commercial connection between the two transactions. Within a span of twenty-three years, businesses developed by a pharmaceutical trading family from Madras had become part of two of India's largest corporate groups, Sun Pharma and Reliance Industries.
