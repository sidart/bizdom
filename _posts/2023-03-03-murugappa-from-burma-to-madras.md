At Chennai's bustling Parry's Corner stands Dare House, named after John William Dare, a business partner of Thomas Parry. The latter, a Welshman who arrived in Madras in 1788, laid the foundations of EID Parry, one of India's oldest surviving commercial enterprises (read my earlier post, [Mr. Parry of Parry's Cornerwale](https://www.thebizdom.in/mr-parry-of-parrys-cornerwale/)). Dare House now houses the headquarters of the Murugappa Group.

The group's history goes back to 1900, when Dewan Bahadur A.M. Murugappa Chettiar established a money-lending and banking business in Moulmein, Burma. The family belonged to the Nattukottai Chettiar community, whose members were among the principal financiers of Burmese cultivators during the colonial period. The Murugappas subsequently expanded their interests across Malaya, Ceylon, Indonesia and Vietnam.

The economic depression of the 1930s, growing anti-Indian sentiment in Burma and the approaching war prompted the family to transfer a substantial portion of its assets to India. Despite suffering considerable losses during the Japanese invasion, the family managed to rebuild its business through the manufacture of emery paper, steel furniture and safes.

In 1949, anticipating a growing market for affordable personal transport in independent India, the family collaborated with Britain's Tube Investments to establish TI Cycles at Ambattur, near Madras. In 1954 came Carborundum Universal, a joint venture with American and British manufacturers of abrasives. The family returned to its original business of lending in 1978 through Cholamandalam.

Meanwhile, EID Parry was struggling financially. As mentioned in my earlier piece, R. Venkataraman, the then finance minister, helped bring the prospective buyer and sellers together. Murugappa acquired EID Parry in 1981, gaining a substantial presence in sugar and fertilisers.

Often described as southern India's Tata Group, Murugappa's businesses today include Coromandel International, TI Cycles and EID Parry. The acquisition of the latter also brought the Chettiar banking family to Dare House, a building that preserves the names and commercial histories of two nineteenth-century European merchants in Madras.
