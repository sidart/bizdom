In 1963, T.P. Gopalan Nambiar established British Physical Laboratories (BPL) at Palakkad in Kerala to manufacture precision panel meters for the Indian defence forces. The company subsequently diversified into medical electronics, manufacturing instruments such as electrocardiographs, and moved its corporate headquarters to Dynamic House on Bangalore’s Church Street.

The turning point in BPL’s history came in 1982, when India hosted the Asian Games in New Delhi. The introduction of colour television broadcasts by Doordarshan created a new market for television manufacturers. Nambiar ventured into consumer electronics, drawing upon BPL’s existing manufacturing capabilities and its technical association with Japan’s Sanyo Electric.

By the 1990s, BPL had become one of India’s leading consumer electronics brands, with a product range that included televisions, refrigerators, washing machines and audio systems. Its other business interests extended to telecommunications equipment, batteries and electronic components.

The entry of South Korean manufacturers Samsung and LG in the latter half of the 1990s, however, gradually altered the competitive landscape of the Indian consumer electronics industry.
