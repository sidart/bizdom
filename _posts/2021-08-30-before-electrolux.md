In 1901, Swedish designer and industrialist Sven Carlsson, along with a few business associates, established AB Lux in Stockholm. Its principal product was a kerosene lamp, popularly known as the Lux lamp, which found customers among factories and municipalities looking for better lighting arrangements.

However, the increasing availability of electricity gradually reduced the demand for kerosene lamps. In 1916, Lux was acquired by another Swedish company, AB Gasaccumulator (AGA), which specialised in acetylene-based lighting systems for lighthouses.

By then, Lux had ventured into a rather different business, that of vacuum cleaners. Among those associated with the marketing of its vacuum cleaners was Swedish entrepreneur Axel Wenner-Gren. The early machines resembled barrels and weighed around 20 kilograms, making them rather cumbersome for domestic use.

In 1919, AB Electrolux was formed through an arrangement involving Lux and Wenner-Gren's sales company, Svenska Elektron. Two years later, Electrolux introduced the Model V, a horizontal vacuum cleaner mounted on metal runners, making it easier to move around the house.

In 1923, Electrolux acquired Lux from AGA, bringing the former kerosene lamp manufacturer under its ownership.
