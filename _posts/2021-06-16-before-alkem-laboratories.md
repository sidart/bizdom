Samprada Singh was the first graduate of his village, Okri, in the Jehanabad district of Bihar. He wanted to become a doctor but ended up studying commerce at Gaya College, graduating in 1950. He initially took to his family's occupation of agriculture, but successive droughts in Bihar forced him to look for alternative sources of income. Among his early commercial ventures was selling umbrellas.

In 1953, on the advice of a friend, Singh opened a small medical store on Patna's Ashok Rajpath. He subsequently entered the pharmaceutical distribution business, first through Laxmi Pharma and later through Magadh Pharma, established in 1960.

His years in distribution brought him into contact with doctors and executives of pharmaceutical companies such as Abbott and Dolphin Laboratories. Realising the limitations of operating a distribution business from Patna, Singh decided to venture into pharmaceutical manufacturing. His first attempt, through Aristo Labs in 1970, was marred by unsuccessful business partnerships.

In 1973, Singh moved to Bombay and established Alkem Laboratories with his younger brother, Basudeo Narayan Singh, who had left his teaching profession to join the family business.

Alkem initially operated as a pharmaceutical marketing company before setting up its first manufacturing facility at Taloja, near Bombay, in 1978.

Over the following decades, Alkem built a particularly strong presence in the antibiotics market. In 2006, its antibiotic brand Taxim became the first anti-infective drug in India to register annual domestic sales exceeding ₹100 crore.

In December 2015, forty-two years after its establishment, Alkem Laboratories made its debut on the Indian stock exchanges.
