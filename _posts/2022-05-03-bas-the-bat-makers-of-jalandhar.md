When Sachin Tendulkar scored his 50th Test century at Centurion, South Africa, in December 2010, the bat he raised had been manufactured by a Jalandhar-based company called Beat All Sports (BAS).

Like the Kohli family behind [Stag International](https://www.thebizdom.in/stag-from-sialkot-to-meerut/), the founders of BAS had their commercial roots in Sialkot. Brothers Desh Raj and Mulkh Raj Kohli established their sporting goods business in 1935. Following Partition, the family migrated to India, bringing along some of its skilled workers, and established its manufacturing unit in Jalandhar in 1950.

Interestingly, the family produced several sportsmen of its own. Mulkh Raj's son Sushil Kohli represented India in swimming and water polo, winning a bronze medal at the 1982 Asian Games in New Delhi. He received the Dhyan Chand Award in 2011. His son, Taruwar Kohli, was part of India's victorious 2008 Under-19 Cricket World Cup team, captained by Virat Kohli.

BAS had been supplying bats to Tendulkar since his teenage years. Its other customers included Sourav Ganguly, M.S. Dhoni, Hashim Amla, Darren Sammy and Alviro Petersen.

For much of its history, BAS remained relatively unknown to television audiences, as the logos displayed on cricketers' bats belonged to their sponsors. At the 2011 World Cup, the company began promoting its own brand more visibly. During India's quarter-final against Australia, Tendulkar even sported the BAS logo on the back of his bat.

In 2013, the company handcrafted two special bats for Tendulkar ahead of his 200th and final Test match.
