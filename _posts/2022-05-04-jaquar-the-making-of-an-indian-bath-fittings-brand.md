In 1960, N.L. Mehra, a small textile trader, ventured into the manufacture of bath fittings from a 60-square-metre unit in Delhi's Walled City. Import restrictions introduced during India's Second Five-Year Plan had created opportunities for domestic manufacturers in an industry where European brands such as Armitage and Shanks catered to the premium segment.

Mehra marketed his products under the Essco brand, offering a rather unusual seven-year warranty on bath fittings. In 1972, Essco established its first organised manufacturing facility.

By the mid-1980s, differences between Mehra and his business partner over expansion led to a division of the business, with both parties retaining rights to the Essco name. To avoid confusion among dealers, the next generation of Mehras — Rajesh, Ajay and Kishan — introduced a new brand in 1986. The name Jaquar was derived from Jai Kaur, their grandmother's name.

Initially, dealers were reluctant to stock the unfamiliar brand. The Mehras invested in consumer advertising, extended the warranty to ten years and appointed service technicians alongside their sales staff. Jaquar's flush valves subsequently became one of its most successful products.

In 2001, Jaquar introduced its Orientation Centres, where customers could experience bathroom fittings before purchasing them. The company subsequently entered sanitaryware, shower enclosures and lighting.

The old Essco name survives as the group's affordable bath fittings brand, while Jaquar caters to the premium segment and Artize, introduced in the early 2010s, to the luxury bathroom market.
