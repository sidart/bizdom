In December 1764, a young Scotsman named John Forbes, originally from Aberdeenshire, arrived in Bombay. His family connections had helped him secure employment as a purser aboard ships of the East India Company. In 1767, he established Forbes & Company, subsequently becoming one of Bombay's select group of ‘Free Merchants’.

Besides trading in Indian cotton and spices, Forbes developed a substantial business in financing the private commercial ventures of the East India Company's civil servants and military officers. The firm also commissioned ships from Bombay's Parsi shipbuilders, particularly the Wadia family. By 1795, Forbes & Company owned, either independently or in partnership, four merchant vessels with a combined tonnage equivalent to nearly a third of Aberdeen's entire merchant fleet in 1787.

Forbes, popularly known back home as ‘Bombay Jock’, returned to Britain in 1796, leaving the business in the hands of his nephew Charles Forbes. Between 1803 and 1805, the firm advanced £2.48 million to the East India Company's Bombay administration.

When John died in 1821, his personal fortune, excluding his landed estates, was estimated at nearly £350,000. Charles subsequently became the first Baronet of Newe. Bombay's Town Hall still houses his marble statue, executed by the celebrated British sculptor Francis Chantrey and financed through public subscriptions.

Through various commercial alliances, the management of Forbes passed from the founding family to the Campbells and subsequently to the Tata Group. An earlier association with the Swedish Match Company helped establish the Western India Match Company (WIMCO).

Another Swedish collaboration came in 1982, when Forbes & Campbell joined hands with Electrolux to establish Eureka Forbes. The company popularised vacuum cleaners through door-to-door demonstrations, followed by the introduction of Aquaguard water purifiers in 1984. Its sales representatives came to be known as Eurochamps.

Eureka Forbes became part of the Shapoorji Pallonji Group in 2002. Two decades later, Advent International acquired a controlling stake through its investment vehicle, Lunolux.

Incidentally, Bombay once had a Forbes Street, named after John Forbes. It was subsequently renamed V.B. Gandhi Marg, after the Indian businessman and social reformer Vithal Balkrishna Gandhi. The old name survives in One Forbes, an office building in Mumbai's Fort area.
