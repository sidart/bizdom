In the 1970s, brothers Ramesh and Kumar Taurani were engaged in the business of trading gramophone records on Bombay’s Lamington Road. Over the years, they became prominent dealers for some of the biggest names in the Indian music industry, including HMV, Music India and CBS.

The brothers soon realised that there was more money to be made in owning music recordings than in merely distributing them. They started recording songs and bhajans by lesser-known singers in Hindi, Sindhi and Marathi, releasing them under their own music label, TIPS. The name stood for ‘To Introduce Promising Singers’.

Their entry into the music recording business coincided with the growing popularity of audio cassettes in India. The relatively inexpensive cassette players and tapes brought recorded music within the reach of a much larger population.

In 1988, the brothers established Tips Cassettes and Records Company and ventured into acquiring Hindi film music rights. Their first acquisition was the soundtrack of *Gunahon Ka Devta*, a film released in 1990, starring Mithun Chakraborty.

Over the next decade, TIPS built a substantial catalogue of Hindi film music, including the soundtracks of *Raja Hindustani*, *Pardes*, *Rangeela* and *Taal*, some of the biggest musical successes of the 1990s.
