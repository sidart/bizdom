In 1968, Canadian-American novelist David Morrell began writing a novel about a Vietnam War veteran struggling to adjust to civilian life. While studying at Pennsylvania State University, Morrell had encountered several young Americans who had returned from Vietnam and learnt about their difficulties in readjusting to life back home.

Morrell was still looking for a suitable name for his protagonist when his wife brought home some apples she had purchased from a roadside vendor in Pennsylvania. He liked the taste and asked her what variety they were. The answer was Rambo, an old American variety of apple. Morrell had also been influenced by the French poet Arthur Rimbaud, whose surname sounded remarkably similar.

The novel, titled *First Blood*, was published in 1972. A decade later, Sylvester Stallone played Rambo in its Hollywood adaptation. The filmmakers changed the ending of the original novel, in which Rambo dies, allowing the character to return in subsequent films.

The name of a rather obscure variety of apple thus became part of popular culture worldwide.
