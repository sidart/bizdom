One of Chennai's most famous establishments serving idli-sambar owes its origins to a family from Mathura, Uttar Pradesh. In 1948, the Gupta family established Ratna Cafe on Triplicane High Road, not far from the centuries-old Parthasarathy Temple. Triloknath Gupta ran several South Indian restaurants, of which Ratna Cafe became the sole survivor.

At Ratna Cafe, customers pay for the sambar, which comes with idlis and unlimited refills. Waiters carry specially designed vessels to pour generous quantities of sambar over the idlis.

In 2002, owing to ill health, Triloknath Gupta contemplated closing the restaurant. His nephew Rajendra Gupta, who was running Picnic Hotel near Ripon Building, acquired the business. He replaced the traditional wood-fired kitchen with modern cooking equipment, despite initial resistance from the cooks. In 2005, Ratna Cafe opened its first branch outside Triplicane, in T. Nagar.

Much of the credit for maintaining its distinctive taste goes to Kaliaperumal, a cook from Trichy who joined the restaurant in the mid-1970s. He learnt the intricacies of preparing sambar from the Iyer cooks employed there. By 2011, he was preparing nearly 3,500 litres of sambar daily for the restaurant's various outlets.

Despite mastering the newer steam-based cooking equipment, Kaliaperumal remained particularly fond of the flavours produced in the old kitchen, where sambar was prepared over firewood in tin-lined brass vessels.
