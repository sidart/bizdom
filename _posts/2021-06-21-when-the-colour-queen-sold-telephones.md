There were several actresses who went by the name Padma in the early years of Indian cinema. Padma Devi, whose original name was Nilima, began her career as an extra at Kohinoor Film Company, once among India's largest silent film studios.

She was one of the earliest action heroines of Indian cinema, appearing alongside stunt actor Boman Shroff in films such as J.B.H. Wadia's *Dilruba Daku* (1933), also known as *The Amazon*. In 1937, she earned the nickname ‘Colour Queen of India’ after appearing in *Kisan Kanya*, India's first indigenously produced colour film.

Padma Devi also featured in advertisements for the Bombay Telephone Company, established in 1925. The company regularly advertised in *filmindia*, a popular film magazine edited by Baburao Patel.

In those days, telephone companies were trying to persuade affluent Indian households to use the instrument for social conversations, besides its obvious utility during emergencies. Film actresses posing with telephone receivers became a recurring feature of their advertising campaigns.

Another actress who appeared in these advertisements was Pramila, originally named Esther Victoria Abraham, who would go on to win the inaugural Miss India beauty pageant in 1947.

Incidentally, according to a US Department of Commerce report, British India had just 1,25,400 telephone lines in March 1945. Most were operated by the government's Posts and Telegraphs Department.
