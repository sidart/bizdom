On 2 September 2011, when Lionel Messi led Argentina against Venezuela at Kolkata's Salt Lake Stadium, the football being used was manufactured by a Jalandhar-based company, Freewill Sports. Its FIFA-approved Nivia Simbolo had already been used in domestic competitions such as the Santosh Trophy.

Like [Stag International](https://www.thebizdom.in/stag-from-sialkot-to-meerut/) and [Beat All Sports (BAS)](https://www.thebizdom.in/bas-the-bat-makers-of-jalandhar/), Nivia's commercial roots lie in Sialkot. Following Partition, several sporting goods manufacturers from Sialkot relocated to Jalandhar and Meerut, bringing along their manufacturing expertise and, in some cases, their skilled workers.

Nihal Chand Kharbanda began his career as an assistant storekeeper at Uberoi & Company, earning Rs 30 a month. In 1934, he established Freewill & Company to trade in raw materials used in manufacturing sporting goods. The business eventually settled in Jalandhar in 1950.

His son Vijay, a postgraduate in political science, spent considerable time at the National Institute of Sports in Patiala, understanding the equipment requirements of Indian sportsmen. In 1961, he introduced Nivia, combining letters from his father's name, Nihal (NI), and his own, Vijay (VI).

In those days, according to Vijay's son Rajesh Kharbanda, dealers would advise his father to sell footballs bearing the inscription ‘Made in Calcutta’, as they doubted the quality of sporting goods manufactured in Jalandhar.

Among the company's early innovations was a laceless football fitted with an inflation valve. In 1980, Nivia ventured into manufacturing leather football shoes with just two craftsmen, subsequently introducing shoes with moulded soles.

For the Argentina-Venezuela match, Nivia supplied 50 footballs to each team and another 1,000 for distribution among spectators. By 2018, its Ashtang football had replaced Puma and Nike as the official match ball of the Indian Super League and India's national football team, respectively.
