On 21 July 1866, a young Italian named Felice Bisleri, not yet 15, was wounded in the shoulder while fighting alongside Giuseppe Garibaldi's troops at the Battle of Bezzecca during Italy's Third War of Independence. He continued fighting and was subsequently awarded a silver medal for military valour.

After returning to Milan, Bisleri worked at his brother's hat shop and later became a coffee salesman. With no formal training in chemistry, he established a small laboratory at 16 Via Savona, where, in 1880, he developed an alcoholic tonic made from cinchona bark and iron salts. It was marketed as Ferro-China Bisleri.

Bisleri was an early practitioner of mass advertising. His product carried the emblem of a lion with the Latin word *Robur*, meaning strength. In 1892, he even started a medical journal, *Rivista Medica*, which he distributed free of cost to thousands of doctors.

In 1894, Bisleri negotiated the acquisition of mineral water rights at the Angelica spring in Nocera Umbra for 900,000 Italian lire. He promoted the product through advertising and free distribution in hospitals and places facing shortages of drinking water.

Bisleri died in September 1921 at San Pellegrino, another Italian town famous for its mineral water.

The brand made its way to Bombay in 1965, where Bisleri was sold in glass bottles in two variants, Bubbly and Still. Parle acquired the Indian business in 1969. Ramesh Chauhan was primarily interested in its soda business, given the demand for soda from five-star hotels.

It was only after selling his soft drink brands to Coca-Cola in 1993 that Chauhan began devoting greater attention to bottled drinking water.

Incidentally, the original Ferro-China Bisleri brand had passed into the hands of Cinzano and subsequently Diageo. In June 2020, the Italian liquor manufacturer Gruppo Caffo 1915 acquired the brand from Diageo.

Nearly a century after Felice Bisleri's death, his surname is associated with two rather different drinks: an alcoholic tonic in Italy and bottled drinking water in India.
