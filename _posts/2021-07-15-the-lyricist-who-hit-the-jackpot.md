Rajendra Krishan was among the most prolific lyricists of Hindi cinema. His repertoire included popular songs such as *Chup Chup Khade Ho*, *Mere Piya Gaye Rangoon*, *Eena Meena Deeka* and *Govinda Ala Re*. Apart from writing lyrics, he also wrote dialogues and screenplays for several Hindi films.

Away from the film studios, Krishan had another passion: horse racing. A regular visitor to Bombay’s Mahalaxmi Racecourse, he reportedly won a jackpot of nearly ₹49 lakh, an extraordinary sum in those days, earning him the reputation of being one of the wealthiest lyricists in the Hindi film industry.

His winnings also belong to an interesting period in the history of Indian taxation. Until 1972, income from horse racing and several other forms of gambling enjoyed exemption from income tax. The government subsequently brought such winnings within the definition of taxable income, partly to prevent their misuse for converting unaccounted money into legitimate income.

The Finance Act of 1978 went a step further by introducing Section 194BB. Beginning 1 June 1978, bookmakers and licensed racecourse operators were required to deduct tax at source on horse racing winnings exceeding ₹2,500.

The taxman had finally arrived at the racecourse.
